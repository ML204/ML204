<Section label="What is it?">

**Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)** is the Swiss Army knife of Linear Algebra. While standard eigenvalues only work on **square** matrices (and fail on pure rotations), SVD works on **every single matrix in existence—any shape $m \times n$!**

It reveals a stunning geometric secret: **every linear transformation on Earth**—no matter how twisted, rectangular, or sheared—can always be broken into just three ultra-clean steps:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{A} &= \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T \\
&(\text{Rotate} \to \text{Stretch} \to \text{Rotate})
\end{aligned}
$$

</Section>

<Section label="Why we need it">

SVD powers some of the most impactful algorithms across classic ML, data compression, and modern Generative AI:

- **Image Compression & Denoising**: Keeping only the largest $k$ singular values strips away background noise while compressing massive pixel matrices to a fraction of their memory size.
- **Recommender Systems (Collaborative Filtering)**: Factorizes huge sparse `Users × Movies` rating matrices into compact latent user-taste ($\mathbf{U}$) and movie-genre ($\mathbf{V}^T$) vectors.
- **Numerically Stable PCA & Pseudoinverse**: Computes Principal Components directly from raw data matrix $\mathbf{X}$ without squaring condition numbers via $\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X}$, and solves ill-conditioned least-squares via the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation for LLMs)**: Inspires parameter-efficient fine-tuning by updating massive weight matrices $\Delta \mathbf{W} \approx \mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$ using tiny low-rank bottlenecks instead of billions of parameters!

</Section>

<Section label="Explained in detail">

### 1. The 3-Step Geometric Recipe: Rotate → Stretch → Rotate

Take a plain unit circle and mark two perpendicular input vectors $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ on it. Even when matrix $\mathbf{A}$ tilts or shears the plane into a slanted ellipse, SVD always finds an **orthogonal pair** in input space ($\mathbf{v}_1 \perp \mathbf{v}_2$) that lands on the **perpendicular major/minor ellipse axes** ($\mathbf{u}_1 \perp \mathbf{u}_2$)!

![Four-panel SVD transformation sequence: unit circle, V-transpose rotation, Sigma axis scaling, and U output rotation](/images/svd/01_svd_three_steps.png)

Read $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{U}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\mathbf{V}^T\mathbf{x}))$ from **right to left** (the order operations hit $\mathbf{x}$):
1. **$\mathbf{V}^T$ (First Rotation/Reflection)**: Turns space so the special perpendicular directions $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2$ line up neatly along the horizontal and vertical coordinate axes.
2. **$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ (Pure Axis Stretch)**: Scales the horizontal axis by $\sigma_1 \ge 0$ and vertical axis by $\sigma_2 \ge 0$ (turning the circle into a clean axis-aligned ellipse!).
3. **$\mathbf{U}$ (Final Rotation/Reflection)**: Rotates the stretched ellipse into its final tilted orientation in output space, mapping axes onto $\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2$.

<Analogy>
  Imagine tailoring a square piece of clay into an oval badge tilted at $35^\circ$: first rotate the square on a turntable so its fibers face you ($\mathbf{V}^T$), roll it wider lengthwise and thinner sideways ($\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$), and finally turn it on the display table to its finished angle ($\mathbf{U}$)!
</Analogy>

---

### 2. Why SVD Works on ANY Rectangular Matrix ($\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$)

For an arbitrary dataset or weight matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$:

![Color-coded shape breakdown of SVD A = U Sigma V^T holding for arbitrary rectangular m x n matrices](/images/svd/02_svd_matrix_shapes.png)

- **$\mathbf{U}$ ($m \times m$)**: **Orthogonal** matrix ($\mathbf{U}^T\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{I}$) whose columns are the **left singular vectors** $\mathbf{u}_i$.
- **$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ ($m \times n$)**: **Diagonal** rectangular matrix holding the **singular values** $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0$ sorted largest to smallest!
- **$\mathbf{V}^T$ ($n \times n$)**: **Orthogonal** matrix ($\mathbf{V}^T\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}$) whose rows are the transposed **right singular vectors** $\mathbf{v}_i^T$.

#### Concrete Rectangular Worked Example ($2 \times 3$)
Consider $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ ($2$ rows, $3$ columns — eigendecomposition cannot even run here!).

Notice only column 1 is non-zero ($\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$, length $\sqrt{3^2+4^2} = 5$):
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1 &= 5, \quad \mathbf{u}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \\[4pt]
\mathbf{v}_1 &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \\[4pt]
\mathbf{A} &= \sigma_1 \mathbf{u}_1 \mathbf{v}_1^T \\
&= 5 \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

All extra singular values ($\sigma_2 = 0$) vanish! Even a rectangular matrix reveals its exact rank ($r = 1$) and strength ($\sigma_1 = 5$) immediately.

---

### 3. Singular Values vs Eigenvalues

How do singular values relate to eigenvalues if $\mathbf{A}$ isn't even symmetric? Compute $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}$—which is *always* symmetric positive semi-definite!

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A} &= (\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^T\mathbf{U}^T)(\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T) \\
&= \mathbf{V}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\mathbf{V}^T
\end{aligned}
$$

That means:
- The **right singular vectors** $\mathbf{v}_i$ of $\mathbf{A}$ are the **eigenvectors** of $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}$.
- Each **singular value** is literally $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A})} \ge 0$.
- The **left singular vectors** $\mathbf{u}_i$ are the **eigenvectors** of $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^T$.

---

### 4. Low-Rank Approximation & Compression

Because $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is sorted from largest ($\sigma_1$) to smallest ($\sigma_r$), we can rewrite **every matrix** as a sum of simple rank-1 "layers":

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{A} &= \sigma_1 \mathbf{u}_1 \mathbf{v}_1^T + \sigma_2 \mathbf{u}_2 \mathbf{v}_2^T \\
&\quad + \dots + \sigma_r \mathbf{u}_r \mathbf{v}_r^T
\end{aligned}
$$

- Layer 1 ($\sigma_1 \mathbf{u}_1 \mathbf{v}_1^T$) paints the single boldest overall pattern in the matrix.
- Keeping just the top $k \ll r$ terms gives $\mathbf{A}_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T$, proven by the **Eckart–Young Theorem** to be the **mathematically best possible** rank-$k$ approximation!

![Low-rank SVD reconstruction of an 8x8 pixel letter A showing Rank-1, Rank-2, Rank-3 vs Full Rank](/images/svd/03_low_rank_compression.png)

Look at what happens with storage: an $800 \times 800$ grayscale photo holds $640\,000$ numbers. Keeping Rank $k = 40$ stores only $40 \times (800 + 800 + 1) \approx 64\,040$ numbers—a **$10\times$ compression ratio** with barely visible loss!

</Section>

<Section label="Notes & Formula">

<Formula label="SVD Factorization">
  $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^T$
  <br />
  $= \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i \mathbf{u}_i \mathbf{v}_i^T$
</Formula>

<Formula label="Orthogonality & Energy Ratio">
  $\mathbf{U}^T\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{I}_m, \quad \mathbf{V}^T\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_n$
  <br />
  $\text{Energy}(k) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i^2}$
</Formula>

    { symbol: 'Vᵀ',     meaning: 'n × n orthogonal matrix of right singular vectors (input directions)' },
    { symbol: 'σᵢ',     meaning: 'i-th singular value: √λᵢ(AᵀA), geometric semi-axis stretch factor' },
    { symbol: 'Rank k', meaning: 'Number of top singular layers retained for compression / denoising' },
  ]}
/>

<Callout kind="tip">
  **Rule of Thumb in NumPy (`np.linalg.svd(A, full_matrices=False)`)**: For a tall $10\,000 \times 20$ data matrix, `full_matrices=True` tries to build a giant $10\,000 \times 10\,000$ matrix $\mathbf{U}$ (800 MB!). Passing `full_matrices=False` (called **Thin/Economy SVD**) returns only the useful $10\,000 \times 20$ columns, running orders of magnitude faster!
</Callout>
